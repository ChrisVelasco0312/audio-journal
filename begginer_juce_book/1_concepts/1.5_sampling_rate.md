ADC Analog to digital, take voltage reading every few microseconds and store
that voltage into 16-bit or 24-bit integer.

The number of bits determines how large the integer can be.

ADC takes an analog, continuous signal that is made up of changing voltage.
and turns it into digital discrete signal consisting of a stream of integer sample values.

Host app maps the integers into floating-point numbers between -1 and +1.

A **theorem** (the Nyquist Theorem) says **sampling rate**
or the speed at which the ADC takes these voltage reading
MUST be at least **twice** the **highest frequency** in the signal being sampled.
If sample rate is not high enough
original signal will not be faithfully represented.

Human hearing tops out at 20000 Hz or 20kHz
If we take 20kHz as upper limit of the frequencies
sampling rate should be at leat 40kHz

48kHz and 44.1kHz sampling rate is enough to capture all sounds
humans can hear

Use of high sampling rate applying oversampling. 
which helps to reduce **aliasing**
An **alias** is a frequency too high to represent with the chosen sample rate
and gets reflected as a lower frequency.
Aliases show up as inharmonic buzzing sounds.
